# A live voice session excludes browser jobs on its browser, and the reverse

A live voice session owns its browser environment for its whole life: the virtual microphone and speaker monitor are instance-wide, and provider voice is a tab in that browser that the gateway's voice control jobs drive. A text or file job on the same browser would switch tabs, change provider state or touch the audio under a live call, and a session started under a running job would do the same to it (the unmet P1 finding of the zcode review). So the two are mutually exclusive on the browser, and nothing else about jobs changes: jobs on one browser still order themselves through the Daemon Pool's one-active-job-per-physical-session rule (ADR-0011). The shared name is the Browser Lane, `topology.BrowserLaneKey`: the host and port of a CDP endpoint, tenant-free, so a topology instance's reported endpoint (`ws://Browser:9222/devtools/...`) and the gateway env's `UBAG_REMOTE_BROWSER_ENDPOINT` (`http://browser:9222/`) are one lane. Jobs carry no account or instance of their own, so the browser is the finest thing the gateway can name for both sides; it is a superset of the same-provider-identity case on the usual one-browser-per-identity topology.

The long-lived half is NOT copied. A session holds its lane through its own lease in the voice store (including the terminating hold that keeps the provider UI reserved while it is torn down), which every replica and every restart already reads; the job consumer asks that store through a probe (`voice.LaneProbe`) and a holder whose lane cannot be worked out counts as holding it (fail closed). Only the transient halves live in `topology.ConcurrencyRegistry`: a job registers on its lane while it runs, and a voice admission registers only around its Reserve or Claim call. Both sides use the same order, register first and look second (the job looks at the voice admissions and the store, the voice side looks at the running jobs before it reserves and releases its registration only after the store call returns), which makes the exclusion race-free without a lock across two stores: a job that slipped past the voice side's look registered after it, so its own look comes after the voice registration and sees either the admission still in flight or the committed lease. Registrations are process-local counts; with a shared (postgres) voice store they move into the shared admission store as expiring tokens, renewed by the job's heartbeat, so another replica sees them and a crashed gateway frees the lane within 90 s.

A job held back by a session goes back to the queue after a delay, exactly like a pool overload (ADR-0011): checked before the job is assigned, so it stays queued; the exec lease is released first; the lease is held for 2 s and then retried; never failed, never run, never re-queued at once. A voice session that finds a running job on its browser is not offered that placement and queues, and `connect` answers the documented 409 "no environment free" while the job runs. The gateway's own voice control jobs (the reserved `voice.` command prefix), targets that do not drive the shared browser (mock, SDK, CLI) and jobs with no remote browser endpoint are never held back. `UBAG_VOICE_LANE_EXCLUSION` is on whenever voice sessions are configured and `0` switches it off; it is on because it changes nothing while no session holds a browser and it is what makes enabling live voice safe. Known limits: a held job sits at the head of the queue, so on the serial consumer it also delays jobs behind it that do not need the browser (the same head-of-line limit as ADR-0011; the helper placer P4.17 supersedes it); a long call pauses text jobs on a one-browser deployment, so dedicate a browser to voice when both must flow; and a deployment that gives jobs and voice different spellings of one browser that differ in host or port (a proxy alias) is not recognised as one lane.
